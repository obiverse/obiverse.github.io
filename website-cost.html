How much does a website cost in Nigeria?

With us, ₦400,000 for a business website, or ₦800,000 with online payments, and ₦25,000 a month to keep it running. Here is what that buys, what makes any website cost more or less, and what to ask before you pay anyone.

Outside Nigeria the same work is $1,000, $2,000 and $50 a month. You pay 60% to start and 40% at go-live.

What changes the price

How many pages, and what is on them

A single page that says who you are is a small job. Products, a factory, distributors and a quote form are a bigger one. Ask any designer how many pages the price covers, and who writes and photographs what goes on them.

Whether it takes money

Taking payments means connecting a payment provider, an admin panel to manage what is sold, and testing that the money lands where it should. That is the difference between our two prices.

Whether it is designed for you

Every site we build carries your brand. A design made from nothing for your business is part of the ₦800,000 option.

What happens after launch

Every website needs hosting and a domain, paid every year, and someone to change it when your prices or products change. Ask whether that is in the price or a surprise in twelve months. Ours is the ₦25,000 care plan.

Before you pay anyone

Six questions to ask

  • Whose name is the domain in? It should be yours.
  • Will it show on Google and on Google Maps? Customers search before they call.
  • Can a customer reach you in one tap on WhatsApp? That is where most conversations in Nigeria happen.
  • What does it cost in the second year? Hosting, domain and updates.
  • When do you pay? Ours is 60% to start and 40% when the site is live and in use.
  • Can you see websites they built that are live today? Ours are below.
